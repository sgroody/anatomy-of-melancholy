Reader A/B round 3

Burton’s in-line citations move to the margin, leaving a mark in the text. The rules found 924 of them in the running text: 652 to authors and 272 to Bible books. Hover a mark or its margin note. Burton’s own words for the citation show on hover in every option. Below that, the rendered initials.

C1What stands in the text where a citation was

The hard case is grammar. “as Avicenna lib. 3 Fen 1 Tract. 4 cap. 18 holds” must not become “as ◦ holds”. Options C and D keep the author’s name in the line, since it is part of Burton’s sentence, and move only the reference.

A · the whole citation goes; a raised § marks the place

… and hot, as § holds, it is a cause of the disease. ’Tis all for your sins,§ God is angry, punisheth, and threateneth…

citesAvicenna, Canon, book 3, fen 1, treatise 4, chapter 18
BibleHag. 1:9–10 Ye looked for much, and, lo, it came to little…

The cleanest line, but “as § holds” reads oddly where the name was the subject.

B · the whole citation goes; a small ¶-like sign stays on the line

… and hot, as ⁋ holds, it is a cause of the disease. ’Tis all for your sins, ⁋ God is angry, punisheth, and threateneth…

citesAvicenna, Canon, book 3, fen 1, treatise 4, chapter 18
BibleHag. 1:9–10 Ye looked for much, and, lo, it came to little…

Like A, but the mark sits on the line at full size, so it is easier to see and to hit, at the cost of a busier line.

C · the name stays, the reference goes; a raised § after the name

… and hot, as Avicenna§ holds, it is a cause of the disease. ’Tis all for your sins,§ God is angry, punisheth, and threateneth…

citesCanon, book 3, fen 1, treatise 4, chapter 18
BibleHag. 1:9–10 Ye looked for much, and, lo, it came to little…

The name keeps its hairline (R5 C) and its index window. A Bible reference has no name to keep, so only the mark stays.

D · the name stays, the reference goes; a small slate dot

… and hot, as Avicenna holds, it is a cause of the disease. ’Tis all for your sins, God is angry, punisheth, and threateneth…

citesAvicenna, Canon, book 3, fen 1, treatise 4, chapter 18
BibleHag. 1:9–10 Ye looked for much, and, lo, it came to little…

The quietest mark. It is set apart from Burton’s lettered notes by shape as well as colour.

In every option the mark takes the citation colour (slate), so it never looks like one of Burton’s lettered notes (rubric). A reading option can put the citations back in the line exactly as Burton printed them.
