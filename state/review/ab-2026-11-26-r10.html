AB-5 · Homepage Copy Rewrite & Spacing Polish

Tightening the infobox prose by ~20% while giving newcomers a clearer sense of Burton’s warmth, humor, and labyrinthine wit:

Current Infobox Prose

The Book In 1621 an Oxford scholar calling himself Democritus Junior set out to anatomize sadness, and he ended up writing about everything: love, air, diet, scholars, religion, the madness of the whole world. Robert Burton’s Anatomy of Melancholy is one of the great books in English, and one of the hardest to read. Every page is thick with Latin and dotted with Greek, and the margins are crowded with citations in a shorthand no living reader was taught.

The Reader This edition is built on Angus Gowland’s text (2021), which follows the sixth edition of 1651. It gives translations of the Latin and Greek, spells out Burton’s citations, identifies the people and books he names, and sets the thousands of notes in the margin beside the passages they explain. Anything a machine produced is marked as such.

Proposed · Tighter, Warmer, Clearer HierarchyRecommended

The Book In 1621, an Oxford don calling himself Democritus Junior set out to write a medical treatise on melancholy—and ended up writing an encyclopaedia of human folly: love, stars, diet, ghosts, scholars’ miseries, and the cure of sorrow. Burton’s prose is hilarious, tender, and notoriously dense with untranslated Latin, Greek, and abbreviated marginalia.

This Reader Built faithfully on Angus Gowland’s text of the 1651 sixth edition, it brings every translation, expanded citation, and biographical note quietly into the margins beside Burton’s words. Any machine-generated help stays visibly marked with ◇ on its reading page.

AB-9 · Worth Borrowing from Your Friend’s 7 Reader Links + Design Critique

Broad take: our edition already executes Tufte-style parallel margins and dark/light typography better than almost every literary web edition — our main friction now is margin visual noise (too many repeated small-caps labels and long chains) and context switching when following links in 50+ page chapters. Here is what each of the 7 sites teaches us, plus 3 interactive prototypes you can pick:

Site StudiedCore PrincipleVerdict for Burton
Tufte CSSAbundant margins, low ink chrome, letting typography signal note roles instead of heavy labels.Adopt (S1): Drop redundant NOTE / GLOSS small-caps headers when layout/italic lemma already signals it.
Power Moby-DickClean 19th-c. serif book column with parallel glosses & zero jarring modal popups.Already beaten on desktop; validates our calm margin column and quiet hover-linking.
The Joyce ProjectSemantic subtle cue colors by annotation type + rich non-linear allusion popups.Keep our restraint: our oxblood/cite/gloss colors work; avoid rainbow prose links.
Andy Matuschak’s NotesHorizontal sliding panes keep your chapter open beside the reference you clicked (zero context loss).Adopt (S2): Optional in-page Side Peek pane when clicking cross-refs or author links in long chapters.
STEPBibleWord-order interlinear + hover context + side lexicon panel for Greek/terms.Adopt (S2/S3): Clickable side/inline term inspection without leaving the chapter.
Nicky Case’s NutshellRecursive, expandable in-place explanations (click a link to unfold context inline).Adopt (S3): Expandable humoral & apothecary chips (atra bilis, per consensum, ℞, ß) inside cards.
