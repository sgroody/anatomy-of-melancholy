R2When Burton translates his Latin himself: four more ways

“wonder of Nature” is Burton’s English for audacis naturae miraculum, though he leaves out audacis (“daring”). Hover the Latin or the English in each.

A · his English takes the Latin’s colour, in his own type

the principal and mighty work of God, wonder of Nature, as Zoroastes calls him; audacis naturae miraculum, the marvel of marvels, as Plato…

The colour already means “a translation goes here” (T7). Here it marks a translation Burton made himself.

B · a faint wash joins the pair, even across words between

the principal and mighty work of God, wonder of Nature, as Zoroastes calls him; audacis naturae miraculum, the marvel of marvels, as Plato…

It reads as one unit. Where they stand far apart, only the two ends get the wash.

C · nothing in the line; the margin says so

the principal and mighty work of God, wonder of Nature, as Zoroastes calls him; audacis naturae miraculum, the marvel of marvels…

Burton’s own“wonder of Nature”, leaving out audacis, daring

The margin slot where a translation would go tells you he has translated it, and where.

D · nothing visible; hover the Latin to light his English

the principal and mighty work of God, wonder of Nature, as Zoroastes calls him; audacis naturae miraculum, the marvel of marvels…

The cleanest page. You only learn the pairing by hovering.

A and C combine well: colour in the line, with the margin noting what he left out. Put “A + C” in the note if you like that.

So the split matters: Gowland’s endnotes alone outweigh everything of Burton’s together. Three ways to divide them:

Burton’s notestranslationsGowland’s notescitations
A · by author: Burton left, Gowland right
left33 %

Left: Burton’s notes, restored notes and their translations. Right: Gowland’s notes and his translations of the text. Clear, but lopsided.

B · by job: translations left, commentary right
left45 %
right55 %

Left: Burton’s notes and every translation (of notes and of the text). Right: Gowland’s glosses and explanations. The sides stay meaningful (left “what it says”, right “what it means”) and nearly even.

C · by room: each note goes to the side with space near its words
left≈ 50 %
right≈ 50 %

The most even and the least drift from the anchor. The side means nothing, so the kind labels (T3 A) carry it all.

My suggestion is B, with C’s rule as the overflow: when one side is full near a note’s words, the note crosses to the other side rather than drifting down. Totals are whole-book. Dense pages differ, which is what the overflow is for.
