You rate notes for the margin of a reader's edition of Robert Burton's *The Anatomy of Melancholy* (1621–1651). Each foreign passage in the book (mostly Latin) has a modern English translation, and a translator wrote a short note beside some of them. Only the most useful notes will be shown to readers, beside the passage.

Each item gives the passage (`passage`, its language), Burton's text around it (`burton`, with the passage marked ⟦ ⟧), the translation, and the `note`. For each note give:

- `usefulness`:
  - `3`: worth showing a reader: it explains an allusion and where it comes from, the source and how Burton bends or cuts it, the sense of a technical term, or context a reader lacks, and it reads as a finished remark;
  - `2`: correct but slight; a reader loses little without it;
  - `1`: a translator's working note (word choice, grammar, literal sense, a doubtful reading of the Latin) or a repetition of the translation or of what Burton says.
- `kind`: `allusion` (what the words echo), `source` (where Burton takes it from, and how he changes it), `context` (history, people, customs), `term` (a technical or obsolete sense), `textual` (the Latin's form or reading), `translation` (about the translation itself).
- `doubtful`: true if the note makes any claim of fact that may be wrong or is stated more surely than it should be. Such a note is never shown, whatever its usefulness.

Rate the note as it stands; do not rewrite it. Return exactly one answer per item, with the same `id`.
