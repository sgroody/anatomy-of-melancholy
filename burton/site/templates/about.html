About

Robert Burton’s The Anatomy of Melancholy (1621, here in the sixth edition of 1651 as edited by Angus Gowland for Penguin Classics, 2021{% if profile == "public" %}, without Gowland’s editorial matter{% endif %}) dissects melancholy through everything he had read. Everything this reader adds sits quietly at the page’s edge—below, the page explains itself in its own margins (point at any phrase or card to light its pair).

How to read Burton here

Burton argues in two voices: nimble seventeenth-century English and a tide of verba antiqua (with μελαγχολία and Old English beside it). Every translation keeps its language’s colour so it never masquerades as Burton’s own prose; his own notesa carry a rubric rule beside his words, while Author trans. flags places where he Englishes the Latin himself. Restored 1638/nineteenth-century notes appear in grey behind ° when enabled.

Abbreviated references stay off the sentence: the authority’s name remains in prose, a quiet § holds the locus in the margin (and KJV Bible verses unfold inline), and any underlined Galen or cross-reference opens an in-page card on hover or opens cleanly in a Side Peek pane on click without losing your spot. {% if profile == "private" %}On wide windows, Gowland’s commentary stands in the left margin, marked in colour without shouting.{% endif %} You can tune spacing, quiet margins, or return to As printed (1651) anytime under Reading options (or Aa).

Finding your way

From the front page, Search scans Burton’s text, notes, and (optionally) translations offline once loaded; Florilegium collects celebrated passages to dip into, and Sortes Burtonianae opens a page at random. The Argument of the Frontispiece pairs every engraving panel with its stanza; Sources indexes every cited authority; Glossary collects humoral terms, apothecary signs, and shorthand locators; and Index preserves Burton’s 1624 alphabetical Table{% if profile == "private" %}, linked page by page to the Penguin text{% else %} (page links omitted in the public build){% endif %}.
