About the book and this reader

“The best book ever written.” — Nicholas Lezard
“This is one of the indispensable books; for my money, it is the best of all.” — Philip Pullman
“The only book that ever took him out of bed two hours sooner than he wished to rise.” — James Boswell, on Samuel Johnson
“The Anatomy of Melancholy is, by a magnificent and somehow very English irony, one of the great comic works of the world.” — Anthony Burgess
“The most amusing and instructive medley of quotations and classical anecdotes I ever perused.” — Lord Byron
“…the tone of a tirade that, in the midst of its fury, smiles at itself.” — William H. Gass
“His manner is to shroud and carry off his feelings under a cloud of learned words.” — Charles Lamb
“A medical treatise, the greatest indeed written by a layman.” — Sir William Osler
“An excellent book… wherein he hath piled up variety of much excellent learning.” — Thomas Fuller

The Book In 1621, an Oxford scholar calling himself Democritus Junior set out to anatomize sadness—and ended up writing about everything: love, stars, diet, ghosts, and the cure of sorrow. Robert Burton’s book is hilarious, tender, and notoriously hard to finish, thick with Latin, Greek, and archaic allusions.

The Reader This edition keeps his prose unbroken without changing a word: every foreign passage translated beside the text, cryptic shorthand (“Hor.,” “Consil. 23.”) spelled out with his authors identified, and explanatory notes sitting quietly in the margin alongside Burton’s own.

Burton read everything. Now you can read him.
