Burton’s synopses, re-set by the printer’s rules

The printed sheets are not a grid of columns. They follow a few rules a compositor applies by eye, and those rules are what make them read. Re-set as live type, the same rules can also tell each branch how to give way when the window narrows. So one sheet serves the full page, the head of a Partition and the front page, from a wide screen down to a phone, with no sideways scrolling and no separate outline.

Theme
Settled
This form wherever the synopses appear, the front page with its own differences; tall heads turned, as the printer turned them; no ◇ on the synopses; a folded brace says “&c.”; the front page’s Partitions keyed with rubric numerals (I, II, III), folded, so the page fits one screen.
Opening (H2, reworked)
Rest the pointer on a folded brace and it fills with rubric from the beak outward, as a name’s window fills its bar before it stays. When it is full the branch opens one brace deep, its own braces folded. Keep resting on the same brace and it fills again: the branch is now fixed (its brace keeps a faint rubric) and stays when you leave. Rest on still and its inner braces open one at a time, in reading order, each filling before it opens. Move off the brace and the growing stops. Rest on an inner brace to open just that one. A branch opened but not fixed folds a moment after the pointer leaves it. A click opens and fixes at once, or folds a fixed branch; on a touch screen or with the keyboard, a tap or Enter does the same.
On the front page
Rest on a Partition’s name (or its brace): it opens onto the chain of its sheets and stops there, each sheet folded, so the page never runs away; rest on a sheet’s brace to open that sheet.

What the printed sheets do

  • R1 A head that divides is set narrow (“Definition, / Member, / Division,”). A leaf runs out to the edge of the page. Width goes to the words at the ends of the branches, not evenly to the levels.
  • R2 A head stands at the middle of its brace, and the brace spans exactly its members, so the beak points at the head.
  • R3 A brace is built from pieces: two curls, a beak and straight rule between them. A tall brace is a longer rule, not a stretched curl.
  • R4 “Or” is set in the members’ column, in the gap between the two members it divides.
  • R5 A leaf that turns over hangs its second line (“Particular; as Gout, Dropsy, &c.”).
  • R6 Burton’s italic locators (Subsect. 4) and his signs (♈, A, ♊) are the finding aids, so they should be the links. A key stands to the left of the head it marks.
  • R7 When a table runs short of width, the printer gives way branch by branch. He narrows the heads, then turns a tall head on its side (“Secondary, as”, “Not necessary, as Memb. 4 Sect. 2.” on sheet A), and finally starts a new sheet, chained by a sign.

The prototype keeps R1–R6 as they are printed. For R7 each branch compares its width with what its words need. A tall head with room along its brace is turned, as the printer turned it. A branch that still lacks room for its columns sets its head over its members, with a bracket instead of a beaked brace. The deepest branches give way first, so the outer braces keep Burton’s form longest. (A short script does the measuring; without it, CSS container queries give a rougher version of the same rule.) At phone width the same sheet has become a braced outline, made of the same pieces and the same words, with nothing swapped. Folding uses the brace itself. Click a brace (or Tab to it and press Enter) to fold its branch into a small stub with its count; rest on a stub to open it for a look, or click it to open it for good. Pointing at any head inks the braces above it in rubric, which shows the path down to it.

II · At the opening of a Partition

This sits at the head of the Partition’s first page, where the folded “Synopsis” box is now. The first sheet is open. Each later sheet is one line: its sign in a rubric column, Burton’s words for it, and a folded brace with its count. The column of signs (♈ A ♊ ♉ B ♋ C) reads as the chain the printer made. Rest on a sheet’s folded brace to open it; a sign inside a sheet (“See ♈”) opens and goes to the sheet it names. The page then carries on into the text.

III · On the front page

The front page keeps its present shape: the engraving, the title, the way in, and the three synopses folded to one line each, so the whole page fits one screen when it opens. Rest on a Partition’s name (or its brace) and, after a moment, it opens onto the chain of its sheets, each still folded; rest on a sheet’s brace to open that sheet. Move away and they fold again; click to keep one open. The braces joining the Partitions and their sheets are this edition’s, so they are drawn lighter; every other brace and every word is Burton’s.
