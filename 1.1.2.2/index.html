Division of the Body, Humours, Spirits.

Of the parts of the Body, there be many divisions: the most approved is that of aa cites Anat. lib. 1 cap. 18.André du Laurens (Laurentius), Historia anatomica, book 1, chapter 18.Laurentius, out of Hippocrates: which is into parts contained, or containing. Contained, are either Humours, or Spirits.

A humour is a liquid or fluent part of the body, comprehended in it, for the preservation of it; and is either innate and born with us, or adventitious and acquisite. gloss acquisite Acquired w gloss radical Naturally inherent.note which some call Cambium … Ros and Gluten The threefold division of alimentary humours into ‘ros’ (‘dew’), ‘cambium’ and ‘gluten’ originates in the Liber pantegni of Constantine the African (d. c. 1087 CE). According to the conventional medieval doctrine, ros is a form of moisture that runs through the veins, and when it passes through the openings at the end of the veins, it begins to whiten and becomes cambium; when fully whitened, as it is incorporated into the bodily member, it transforms into gluten.note The first concoction in the Liver ‘Concoction’ is the technical term in Galenic medicine for the ‘cooking’ of foodstuffs in the process of digestion. In this understanding, food was first concocted in the stomach and intestines, producing chyle, which was then secondarily concocted in the liver into nutritive blood, then sent via the veins to the other parts of the body to be refined into humours. The third concoction involved the secretion and excretion of various substances, such as sweat, hair and tears, from various parts of the body. Referring to the ‘first’ concoction in the liver is thus slightly misleading.gloss juice Essential bodily fluid (that is, ‘humour’).gloss peccant Unhealthy, corrupt.about Meseraic veins The Mesenteric veins, which carry blood from the intestines to the liver.gloss feculent Containing dregs or impurities.note subtle Very fine or rarefied.
