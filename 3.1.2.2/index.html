Since astral movements were taken to exert influence upon earthly events, the implication here is that everybody and everything is subject to shifts of fate.note Ascendents, Lords of their genitures In astrology, the ‘lord of the geniture’ was whichever planet was the dominant influence at the time of a person’s nativity. Burton refers here to his horoscope, a copy of which (drawn up in his own hand) is found in one of his books now held in the Bodleian Library, and reproduced on his monument in the cathedral of Christ Church in Oxford. It shows that at his birth (8 February 1577), Saturn was at the highest point in the sky and in the sign of Capricorn, and that the ‘ascendant’ (the sign rising on the eastern horizon) was Aries, one of the ‘houses’ (the twelve parts of the zodiac with particular significance in human affairs) which was ruled and at that point occupied by Mars.
