aa note Haec co­mice dicta cave ne male ca­piasTranslation from Latin: from LatinLat.Take care not to take amiss these things said in jest. Burton's sidenote to his Latin poem 'Ad librum suum' (To his book). It is attached to the parenthesis 'nam diligit istas' ('for he loves them', meaning the noble girls who may read the book). The note asks the reader not to take the playful claim that the author loves women in the wrong way. 'Comice' literally means 'in the manner of comedy'..Take care not to take amiss these things said in jest.

/ but none will: eagles do not catch flies. / They have no leisure to spend fleeting time on trifles, / nor do I want such readers; my reader shall be my equal. / If a grave matron should chance to turn aside this way, / a noble lady, or a Countess read you: / there is what may displease them, and what perhaps may please them; Burton's own Latin verses to his book; the sidenote 'Haec comice dicta cave ne male capias' means 'Take care not to misconstrue what is said here in jest.' 'Fungus' (mushroom) is the usual Latin insult for a dolt, here rendered 'blockhead'. The line 'Conveniunt stomacho non minus ista suo' is ambiguous: literally 'these things suit his stomach no less'. It probably means the book agrees no better with the boor than with the rhetorician, though it could mean the opposite, which would lead into the next line, 'yet do not drive him away'. Hence medium confidence. 'Sit lasciva licet pagina, vita proba est' echoes Martial 1.4.8, 'lasciva est nobis pagina, vita proba.' The opening address to the book follows the classical convention of Ovid (Tristia 1.1) and Martial.
