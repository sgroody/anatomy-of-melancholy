Burton cites: Plautus, Pseudolus, Act. 1 Scen. 3 (unverified)The cursing match between Pseudolus (Ps.) and the pimp Ballio (B.), who welcomes every insult. The marked passage begins in mid-exchange: the paragraph opens with 'Ps. Impudice. B. Ita est. Ps. sceleste. B.' ('Shameless! B. So I am. Ps. Villain! B.'), which is outside the marked text. 'Babae' and 'bombax' are mock-exclamations of surprise or delight, left as written; the English equivalent is something like 'Oho!' and 'Hurrah!'. A furcifer was a slave made to carry the fork-shaped yoke as a punishment, so 'gallows-bird'. 'Cantores probos' is ironic: Ballio pretends the abuse is a choir singing his praises. Compared with the standard text of Plautus, Burton leaves out some insults and replies (for example 'bustirape … certo' and 'legirupa … valide'). He also gives 'vera dicis' where Plautus has Ballio reply 'vetera vaticinamini' ('you're prophesying old news'). In the standard text, 'impure leno' is spoken by another character.
