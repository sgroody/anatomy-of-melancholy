Avicenna lib. 3 Fen 1 cap. 23 & 24§ sets down seven compendious ways how this malady may be eased, altered and expelled, Savonarola 9 principal observations, Jason Pratensis prescribes eight rules besides Physic, how this passion may be tamed, Laurentius 2 main precepts, Arnaldus, Valleriola, Montaltus, Hildesheim, Langius, and others inform us otherways, and yet all tending to the same purpose. The sum of which I will briefly Epitomize (for I light my Candle from their Torches), and enlarge again upon occasion, as shall seem best to me, and that after mine own method. The first rule to be observed in subduing this stubborn and unbridled passion, is exercise and diet. trans. To this purpose serve medicines that lull desire to sleep: camphor, for instance, bound to the privy parts and carried in the breeches (says one), makes the member flaccid. A noble maiden laboured under this disease, for whom the physician prescribed, among other things, that she should wear on her back for twenty days a plate of lead pierced with many holes; and to dry up the seed he ordered that she be fed as sparingly as possible and eat often of prepared coriander, and lettuce and sorrel seed; and so he freed her from the disease. Moreover, willow leaves bruised and drunk hinder and abate coition, and if used more often they take it away altogether. A topaz worn in a ring does the same; Alexander Benedictus writes that the right testicle of a wolf, ground and given in oil or rose-water, brings on a loathing of venery; buttermilk eaten, hemp seed, and camphor given do the same. The herb vervain carried about extinguishes lust, and so does the powder of a frog beheaded and dried. To extinguish coition, let the genitals, the kidneys and the pubic region be anointed with water in which Theban opium has been dissolved; camphor is the thing most contrary to lust, and dried coriander breaks coition and hinders the erection of the yard; mustard drunk does the same. Give vervain in drink and the yard will not rise for six days; use dried mint with vinegar; the genitals smeared with the juice of henbane or hemlock allay the appetite for coition, etc. Take of the seeds of lettuce, purslane and coriander, of each 1 drachm; of dried mint half a drachm; of the whitest sugar 4 ounces; let them all be finely powdered, and afterwards mix them together with water-lily water; make a solid confection in small morsels. Let him take one of these in the morning when he rises. You may find almost countless remedies like these in Hildesheim in the place aforesaid, in Mizaldus, Porta, and the rest.
